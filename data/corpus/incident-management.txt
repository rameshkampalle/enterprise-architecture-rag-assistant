1. PURPOSE
This guide defines the minimum process for identifying, classifying, escalating, communicating, and closing production incidents.

2. SEVERITY LEVELS
P1 - Critical
- Complete outage of a critical business service, confirmed major security compromise, or major loss of Restricted data.
- Incident commander must be assigned immediately.
- Operations and relevant security/business owners must be notified within 15 minutes.
- Status updates should be issued at least every 30 minutes while the incident remains critical.

P2 - High
- Severe degradation or outage of an important service with significant business impact and no acceptable workaround.
- Technical owner must be engaged within 30 minutes.
- Status updates should normally be issued at least every 60 minutes.

P3 - Medium
- Partial degradation, limited user impact, or a problem with a viable workaround.
- Handle through the normal support process with appropriate technical ownership.

P4 - Low
- Minor issue, cosmetic defect, or request with little immediate operational impact.
- Handle through normal backlog/support prioritization.

3. INITIAL TRIAGE
Capture the incident start time, affected service, environment, user/business impact, recent deployments or changes, correlation IDs, relevant safe error codes, and known dependencies.
Do not paste passwords, tokens, private keys, full customer records, or Restricted data into the incident ticket.

4. ESCALATION
Escalate immediately to Information Security when there is suspected credential compromise, unauthorized access, unexpected public exposure, malware, or possible loss of Restricted data.
Escalate to the owning development/integration team when repeated technical failures indicate a service or dependency defect.

5. TECHNICAL EVIDENCE
Preserve relevant logs, trace or correlation IDs, timestamps, deployment versions, queue/DLQ status, and monitoring graphs.
Evidence collection must comply with the Data Classification Policy and Logging & Monitoring Standard.

6. RECOVERY AND CLOSURE
Restore service safely before pursuing non-essential optimization.
For P1 and P2 incidents, create a post-incident review that records timeline, root cause, contributing factors, customer/business impact, corrective actions, and owners.
Corrective actions should have due dates and be tracked to completion.

7. COMMUNICATION
Use factual language. Clearly separate confirmed facts, working hypotheses, actions in progress, and next update time.
Do not speculate publicly about security breaches or customer data exposure before Information Security and Communications approve the statement.
